# Business User Guide

This guide is for business users, project sponsors, product managers, and
non-technical stakeholders who need to use the repository to guide software
work.

## What This Repository Gives You

The repository gives you a structured way to define what should be built,
review whether it is ready, and keep delivery aligned with business goals.
You do not need to know every technical detail to use it effectively.

## When A Business User Should Use It

Use this repository when you need to:

- Define a new business need or service.
- Prioritize competing requests.
- Identify stakeholders and ownership.
- Clarify what success means.
- Review whether a solution is ready to ship.
- Understand why a design or release was approved or rejected.

## How To Use It

### Step 1: Start With The Problem

Write a short description of the business problem, who it affects, and why it
matters. Keep it focused on outcomes, not implementation.

### Step 2: Pick The Right Skill

Use the skill that matches the current question:

- Use [skills/define-use-case/SKILL.md](../skills/define-use-case/SKILL.md) to
  frame the opportunity.
- Use [skills/identify-stakeholders/SKILL.md](../skills/identify-stakeholders/SKILL.md)
  to identify owners and contributors.
- Use [skills/define-business-objectives/SKILL.md](../skills/define-business-objectives/SKILL.md)
  to define measurable goals.
- Use [skills/define-success-criteria/SKILL.md](../skills/define-success-criteria/SKILL.md)
  to agree on what success looks like.
- Use [skills/perform-readiness-assessment/SKILL.md](../skills/perform-readiness-assessment/SKILL.md)
  before release.

### Step 3: Review The Output

Look for four things in the output:

1. Clear decisions.
2. Clear rationale.
3. Clear next actions.
4. Clear owners.

### Step 4: Ask For Gaps To Be Closed

If the output shows missing information, request the team to fill the gaps
before moving forward.

## What A Good Output Looks Like

A useful answer from the repository should include:

- A decision or recommendation.
- The reason for that decision.
- The assumptions made.
- Risks or constraints.
- The next action.

## Business Review Checklist

Use this checklist when reviewing a proposal or project update:

- Is the business problem clear?
- Is the target outcome measurable?
- Are the stakeholders identified?
- Is the scope reasonable?
- Are risks visible?
- Is there a readiness plan?
- Is there a clear owner for each next step?

## Using The Repository In Meetings

In a workshop or review meeting, the repository can help you:

- Keep the conversation structured.
- Capture decisions instead of opinions.
- Confirm what must happen before approval.
- Reduce repeated discussion in follow-up meetings.

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

- Starting with solution details before the problem is agreed.
- Accepting vague success criteria.
- Skipping stakeholder identification.
- Treating readiness as an afterthought.
- Approving work without documented next actions.

## Business Terms In Plain Language

- Use case: the business problem and the intended outcome.
- Stakeholder: anyone who influences, uses, or approves the work.
- Success criteria: how the team will know the work was worth doing.
- Readiness: whether the solution is ready to move forward safely.
- Governance: the rules that make sure work is consistent and controlled.

## Practical Example

If a team proposes a new AI support assistant, a business user should ask:

1. What problem is it solving?
2. Who uses it?
3. What outcome is expected?
4. How will success be measured?
5. What must be true before it can go live?

## Escalation Guidance

Escalate when:

- The problem statement is unclear.
- Owners are missing.
- The business outcome cannot be measured.
- The solution introduces high risk without a mitigation plan.
- The team cannot explain why the proposal is ready.
